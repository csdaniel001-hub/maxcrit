# MaxCrit voice guide (Daniel, 2026-11-25)

MaxCrit articles should read like a well-informed player wrote them for other players. Accuracy rules stay exactly as they are. The voice comes from judgment, context and plain talk, never from invented play experience.

## The test
Read the first two paragraphs aloud. If they could be the opening of a wiki entry or a patch-note summary, rewrite them.

## 1. Open with the player's stake
The first sentence says what changes for the reader, or why they should care today. The date, version number and source come after, or not at all.
- Wiki: "Aniimo's November 26 update lowers the Whisperwake Isles requirements."
- MaxCrit: "If you stalled out somewhere around Breezy Plains, Aniimo just made your week easier."

## 2. One angle per article
Decide what the story is before writing: the one change that matters most to most players. Lead with it and give it room. Everything else is supporting detail, shorter and later. Do not follow the announcement's order.

## 3. Say what you think, and make it read as opinion
Editorial judgment is welcome when it rests on verified facts: "Iron Phalanx is the skill to learn first", "the free box is the only part of the Carnival worth logging in for if you don't spend". Phrase it as a judgment, not as a fact or a measurement. Never imply that someone played or tested it.

## 4. Talk to "you"
Write to the player directly. Use "we" for MaxCrit's own work ("we rounded these codes up on..."). Contractions are fine.

## 5. Facts plainly, no attribution tags
Official facts are stated as facts; the sources box carries attribution. Attribute in the text only when it changes how far the reader should trust the claim: reported, unconfirmed or disputed claims, leaks, direct quotes, tracker-reported codes.

## 6. Tables only for tables
Use a table for schedules, server times, numbers to compare or lists of codes. Do not turn prose into a table to look thorough. A list of three skills reads better as two good sentences.

## 7. Rhythm
Mix short and long sentences. Connect paragraphs ("That matters because...", "The catch is..."). One idea per paragraph. Cut any sentence that only restates the previous one.

## 8. End on a takeaway
The last paragraph tells the reader what to do or what to watch for. No closing link dumps, no summary of what was just said.

## What stays banned
Hands-on claims without real play, invented numbers, "we don't know / check the game" filler, em dashes, bold in running text, reader-steering frames ("here's why", "the key point"), "X, not Y" as a habit, stock phrases.

## Review question
After "Is every claim sourced?" comes "Would a person who plays this game write it like this?" A no on either one sends the piece back.
