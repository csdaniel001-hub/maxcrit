A free-to-play Diablo Immortal account can waste months of Legendary Gem progression by putting all its Gem Power into the first five-star gem it finds. The expensive gem may add Resonance, yet its rank can stall when the required duplicate copies become harder to obtain than the generic materials. Meanwhile, cheaper gems can improve several equipment slots and provide effects that directly benefit the build.

The early-November 2026 Auxiliary Gem changes make this decision more flexible. You can preserve an invested primary gem while using the effect of a compatible auxiliary gem at a higher effective rank than was previously possible. That is another reason to plan your investments around specific effects and upgrade costs.

This guide prioritizes sustainable PvE progression for players who spend no money. PvP has different demands, particularly against players with substantially higher Resonance and secondary attributes. There is no universal eight-gem loadout that solves those differences for every class.

## Understand rarity, rank and Gem Power

A Legendary Gem's star category and its upgrade rank describe different things. A one-star gem can reach a high rank, while a gem in the five-star family may drop with only two filled stars. The latter is generally written as 2/5. Quality and rank influence progression, while the gem's individual effect determines how well it fits your skills.

Gem Power is the material used to develop Legendary Gems. Duplicate copies become a separate requirement during progression. Treat Gem Power and duplicate stock as two budgets. Feeding a spare copy of your planned gem into generic Gem Power can leave you able to pay one part of an upgrade but unable to supply the required copy.

Keep matching copies of gems you intend to upgrade in a separate reserve. A duplicate that completes an upgrade can be much more useful than the generic Gem Power it would provide. Blizzard extended Gem Power Extraction and Transfer to one- and two-star gems in its December 2024 update, which provides more flexibility when changing investments.

## Which lower-rarity gems should you consider?

These are four examples of one- and two-star gems with effects suited to different PvE builds. The recommendations below follow from their activation conditions and drawbacks, without ranking them by damage.

| Gem | Rarity | Reason to consider | When to reconsider |
|---|---|---|---|
| Berserker's Eye | 1-star | General damage increase | Incoming damage already forces you to disengage |
| Mother's Lament | 2-star | Temporary Critical Hit Chance buff | Your build cannot make good use of its buff window |
| Pain Clasp | 2-star | Damage against enemies with continuous damage effects | Your targets rarely suffer continuous damage |
| Fervent Fang | 2-star | Repeated attacks against the same enemy | Short encounters finish before stacks build up, or copies are scarce |

Pain Clasp works when the target is suffering continuous damage. A build that reliably applies bleed or poison can maintain that condition across a boss encounter. If you remove the skill that supplies the qualifying effect, Pain Clasp may lose much of its value. Consider the uptime of the enabling effect before choosing the gem.

Mother's Lament rewards aligning high-damage skills with its temporary critical-hit buff. Berserker's Eye provides broader damage coverage at the cost of taking more damage yourself. Fervent Fang builds value over successive hits against an individual target. A player farming small packs may prefer an immediate damage increase, while a player spending most of a raid attacking a single boss has more opportunity to benefit from stacking damage.

For example, suppose your current dungeon build uses a continuous-damage skill but your raid setup replaces it with a defensive skill. Pain Clasp may be a useful choice for the dungeon setup, while Fervent Fang deserves consideration for the raid. If both builds already survive easily, Berserker's Eye is another candidate. Choose around the skills and activity first, then reserve resources for the corresponding gem.

## Plan your upgrades in stages

The first goal is to put useful effects in your unlocked primary equipment sockets. Avoid leaving most slots undeveloped while trying to max one rare gem. Bring affordable candidates to a rank where they contribute to the build, then inspect what the next breakpoint provides. Early ranks can improve an existing build while you learn which skills you want to keep.

Before choosing a premium gem, price the whole route to your next useful rank. Record the Gem Power and matching copies demanded by the upgrade plan separately. In March 2026, Blizzard extended progressive upgrades to one- and two-star gems beginning with the rank-four-to-five transition, bringing their progression in line with five-star gems. This gives players a way to advance through smaller steps.

Consider an account with enough Gem Power for one upgrade but no spare copy of its intended premium gem. Committing all those materials to the premium project will not solve the duplicate shortage. Set aside the missing copy as a long-term goal and put available resources into a useful lower-rarity gem if that improves your immediate progression. If both required resources for the premium gem are already available, completing that upgrade may be the better choice.

A useful planning sheet needs only four columns: gem, current rank, missing upgrade materials and intended activity. Mark each upgrade as ready or blocked. Complete the ready upgrades that help your main build, keep reserved duplicates out of fodder, and revisit blocked upgrades as new copies arrive. This makes the opportunity cost visible.

## Awakening and Resonance need separate planning

Resonance contributes to your character's overall power, while Combat Rating can matter for access to and effectiveness in higher-difficulty PvE. Gem effects may still produce a better practical result for a particular activity. A higher Resonance number alone cannot tell you which of two builds will clear a dungeon faster.

Awakening and Gem Resonance have received separate accessibility updates. Blizzard made Dawning Echoes, the item required for Awakening, purchasable with Platinum in 2024. Later that year, the Gem Resonance system moved to rank four. The March 2026 update also reduced the required level for unlocking Gem Resonance slots by one. Awakening and Resonance sockets serve distinct progression goals.

Plan around the skills affected by Awakening and preserve enough Platinum for your chosen progression path. Spending all your resources on a new premium gem may delay an improvement to an existing equipment slot.

If you are deciding between a new gem and an awakened slot, write down the desired skill effect, current gem rank, required upgrade materials and Platinum needed. Give priority to an upgrade that helps the skills you actually use. Avoid committing to a slot merely because its gem reached an arbitrary rarity threshold.

## Use Auxiliary Gems after the November update

Blizzard's early-November update allows Auxiliary Gems to provide effects above rank one, subject to the gem's quality and the rank of the socketed primary gem. The new maximums are as follows.

| Auxiliary Gem quality | Maximum auxiliary rank |
|---|---:|
| 1-star | 5 |
| 2-star | 6 |
| 2/5 | 6 |
| 3/5 | 7 |
| 4/5 | 8 |
| 5/5 | 9 |

The primary gem's rank is an additional ceiling. If your primary gem is rank four, a rank-five auxiliary effect cannot be active at rank five through that socket. A dormant primary gem can still host a rank-one auxiliary. Blizzard also illustrates a rank-four Haunt Coil in a rank-ten Blessing of the Worthy: the primary retains its Resonance and Combat Rating, while the rank-four Haunt Coil supplies the active affix. Auxiliary Gems do not contribute Gem Power toward the primary's upgrade.

For an example, imagine a rank-seven primary and a compatible 2/5 auxiliary owned at rank seven. The auxiliary's quality limits its active effect to rank six, even though the primary could support rank seven. If the primary is replaced with a rank-four gem, the primary's rank becomes the tighter limit. The examples assume compatibility and sufficient owned auxiliary rank; they are deductions from the stated caps, not client-tested interactions.

This makes an older primary gem worth keeping when its investment remains useful and you want to try a newer effect. The feature does not automatically grant the auxiliary's full rank or make premium duplicates free. For a dedicated auxiliary, stop at its quality cap. Ranks above that cap provide no additional active rank in that slot, and the primary gem may impose a lower ceiling.

## A sustainable free-to-play resource routine

Elder Rifts are a source of Legendary Gems, and events and Battle Pass rewards can provide materials for further progression. Blizzard introduced Telluric Pearls for five-star Legendary Gem crafting and later added their use in certain progressive upgrades. Reserve Pearls for one defined target at a time.

The May 2025 Battle Pass update introduced a rank-40 choice between a two-star gem selection chest and a Telluric Pearl. When choosing between these reward types, compare the immediate value of a needed duplicate with the longer-term value of progress toward a planned five-star gem. A player whose main two-star gem is blocked by a missing copy has a different priority from someone with a completed lower-rarity setup saving for a premium project.

Keep targeted duplicates apart from generic fodder before confirming bulk upgrades. Use a short weekly planning routine: collect earned rewards, update your duplicate reserve, identify your next attainable upgrade, then save any resources that do not advance it. Avoid diverting Pearls into an unrelated gem solely because it becomes available during an event.

## Test effects before making the next major investment

Choose one repeatable PvE activity or a consistent training target. Keep your class, skills, difficulty, equipment and other gems unchanged. Compare two compatible candidate effects using the Auxiliary Gem system. Run several equal-duration tests and record damage, clear time and survivability. A stationary target may favour long ramp-up effects while actual dungeon trash dies too quickly to benefit from them. For that reason, test both sustained and short encounters if those are your regular activities.

The correct investment is usually the one that removes your current bottleneck. If you die often, prioritize survivability and progression requirements. If your party clears trash quickly but struggles on bosses, investigate sustained damage and reliable activation conditions. If your gem is good but you lack copies, leave it at its present rank while developing another slot.
