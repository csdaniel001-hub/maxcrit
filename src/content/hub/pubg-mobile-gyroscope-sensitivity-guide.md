A sensitivity code can make PUBG Mobile feel fantastic on one phone and nearly unplayable on another. Your grip, available thumb travel, display size and comfort with motion aiming determine how much correction you can make before the crosshair runs away from the target. A good setup lets you acquire an enemy, track their movement and correct recoil without repeatedly lifting your thumb or twisting your wrists into an awkward position.

The best way to find that setup is to separate looking, tracking and firing into different tests. Start with the Red Dot and your most-used mid-range scope. You can tune the remaining optics once those two are comfortable. The approach below is a calibration procedure you can run yourself on the standard mobile client, starting from whatever settings you use now.

## What each sensitivity setting controls

Open Settings and look for Sensitivity. Depending on the client version, you may find separate general and weapon-specific settings. PUBG MOBILE also documents quick sensitivity adjustments in Training Grounds, which are useful for making small corrections without leaving practice.

| Setting | What to test | Typical symptom of a bad setting |
|---|---|---|
| Camera | Touch movement while looking or aiming without firing | Overshooting targets during initial acquisition |
| ADS | Touch movement during firing | Running out of thumb travel when controlling a spray |
| Gyroscope | Tilt movement while aiming without firing | Crosshair drifts past a target as you tilt |
| ADS Gyroscope | Tilt response during firing, when separately enabled | Spray climbs or is dragged below the target |
| Free Look | Looking around independently | Uncomfortable scanning while moving |

Gyroscope Off uses touch for aim correction. Scope On (sometimes described as activating the scope) adds motion input during scoped aiming. Always On extends it to normal camera movement as well. Beginners can start with Scope On to learn how the phone reacts without having every movement affect their crosshair. Always On makes sense if you want motion correction during close-range fights, where you may never open an optic.

## Choose a starting point

Keep your current setup if it already works, and tune from there. If you are starting fresh, begin from the game's default values and change one slider at a time. Sensitivity values are percentages on the game's sliders, and the same number can feel very different on a small phone and a large tablet, so a code copied from another player is only a starting point.

If gyro is Off, ignore the gyroscope rows. If Always On is new to you, lower the motion values until you can keep a stationary target centred. Do not change your HUD, graphics preset and sensitivity at the same time: you won't know which adjustment helped.

## Build a repeatable test in Training Grounds

Equip an M416 with your usual attachments and choose a fixed target distance. Use the same magazine size, stance, optic and frame-rate setting throughout a comparison. The M416 is a useful reference because it gives you a repeatable assault-rifle drill; it does not need to be your main weapon. Use your regular grip and fire button position. If you set sensitivity while resting your phone on a desk but play while holding it unsupported, the result may be misleading.

Start at a distance where the target is clearly visible, such as 20 metres for Red Dot testing. Aim at the same point before each magazine. Shoot five magazines per setting, record how many rounds land within your chosen target region, and also record whether the remaining shots end above, below or beside it. The hit region must stay the same throughout the experiment. If the target does not supply an exact hit count, compare screenshots of the resulting groups instead. You are looking for a repeatable trend across attempts.

Keep a simple log for each comparison:

| Trial | Setting changed | Shots in region (out of 30) | Where the rest landed |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | | | |
| B | | | |
| C | | | |

Repeat each trial several times before picking a winner. A lucky magazine can hide a poor setting, so take breaks if fatigue changes your grip.

## Tune camera and gyroscope before shooting

Pick two stationary targets. Alternate between them ten times using your regular aim input without shooting. If the crosshair consistently stops beyond the second target, reduce the relevant Camera or Gyroscope slider. If you stop short and need another large swipe or tilt, increase it. Make changes of roughly five percentage points for touch or ten to twenty for gyro, then repeat. These are adjustment increments you can shrink as you approach a comfortable value.

Next track a moving target for several seconds. Aim for smooth corrections that keep the crosshair near its centre. A twitchy crosshair that oscillates around the target suggests too much sensitivity for your current control technique. Change only the setting responsible for that input. With gyro enabled, deliberately practise a touch-only pass and a motion-only pass so you can tell which input produces the unwanted movement.

Once you can find and track targets without firing, leave those settings alone and start the firing test. This prevents you from compensating for a bad camera setting by changing recoil control.

## Tune firing sensitivity for each optic

For touch-only play, fire while dragging down at a natural speed. If you repeatedly reach the lower edge of your available thumb area before the magazine ends, try increasing ADS sensitivity a little. If modest corrections pull the aim well below the target, lower it. The purpose is to make your comfortable physical motion sufficient for the correction you need.

For gyro play, use a small controlled tilt to compensate for upward recoil. Increase ADS Gyroscope if you run out of comfortable tilt while the aim continues climbing; decrease it if small movements send your aim down too far. Separate ADS and normal gyro settings also let you keep a steady view before firing while providing stronger correction during a spray.

At 3x or 4x, move the target farther away and repeat. A value that feels excellent with Red Dot can be too abrupt with a magnified optic. For 6x or 8x, test carefully controlled taps and short strings at a distance that suits your normal use. Higher magnification makes small corrections more noticeable. Recoil varies with the weapon, attachments and stance, so a tighter M416 group does not prove your AKM or Beryl M762 will behave identically.

## When to use weapon-specific settings

Leave general sensitivity in place while learning. If your client has Advanced Sensitivity entries for individual weapons, inspect those after you have a stable general profile. A weapon-specific override is useful when the general 3x setting works on one rifle but consistently fails on another under otherwise comparable conditions.

Record both configurations. If you change the general slider and nothing seems to happen on your favourite rifle, check whether that rifle has its own override. Also check that you changed the firing slider if the problem occurs during firing. Retest in an ordinary unranked encounter before relying on a new setup in ranked play.

## Save and import sensitivity codes safely

Use the cloud or sharing controls on the Sensitivity screen if your client exposes them. Save your original settings and take screenshots of the key scope rows before importing a new code. Review the preview before confirming, then check gyro mode, separate firing settings and any weapon-specific overrides. Exact button labels can change between client versions and regional editions.

An imported code is a configuration shortcut. It cannot remove recoil, eliminate random horizontal deviation or guarantee that a particular gun will stay on a distant enemy. Never use third-party recoil scripts or modified clients. The publisher specifically warns against bypassing emulator matchmaking; if you play on a computer, see our [GameLoop PUBG Mobile guide](/hub/guides/gameloop-pubg-mobile-guide.html) and follow the current account rules.

## Troubleshooting an inconsistent setup

| Symptom | First thing to check |
|---|---|
| Aim feels fine until the trigger is pressed | Separate firing sensitivity |
| Aim drifts without touching the screen | Gyro mode, grip and device sensor behaviour |
| One rifle feels wrong with an otherwise good optic | Weapon-specific overrides and attachments |
| Training feels smooth but matches stutter | Frame-rate stability, heat and network conditions |
| Sensitivity feels different after importing a code | Scope rows, gyro toggles and overrides |

If the device becomes hot or frame pacing deteriorates, stop the comparison and allow it to return to normal operating conditions. A high frame-rate cap that the phone cannot maintain may produce inconsistent feedback. A sensitivity slider cannot repair stuttering, latency or a malfunctioning motion sensor.

Keep the final settings unchanged for a few sessions. If a recurring problem survives that practice, adjust only the affected optic and input category. For current content and balance changes, check our [PUBG Mobile 4.7 patch notes](/hub/patch-trackers/pubg-mobile-4-7-patch-notes.html). Retest your main weapon after a relevant balance update or a change of phone. The goal is a stable, repeatable setup that survives actual matches.
