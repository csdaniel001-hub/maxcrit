Crown Series is Clash Royale's tournament mode. Every match is a best-of-3 Duel played with a Duel Deck: 3 or 4 eight-card decks that can't share a single card, so you need several decks from one collection. This guide covers how the mode is structured and how to turn your collection into a lineup that holds up across a whole weekend.

## Unlocking and placement

Crown Series opens at Arena 17 with 6,000 Trophies. There are 13 Leagues: Leagues 1 to 8 each have their own name (Dagger, Pancake, Mustache, Magic, Hammer, Armor, Phantom and Queen's), and Leagues 9 to 13 together form the King's League.

Your League is recalculated at the start of each tournament from your Trophy Road progress, your best Ranked and Ultimate Champion finishes, and your previous Crown Series Duel results. During regular Duel Weekends, King's League players can be matched against anyone else in the King's League; that doesn't apply on Grand Duel Weekends.

Each League caps card levels:

| League | Card level cap |
|---|---:|
| 1–2 | 11 |
| 3–4 | 12 |
| 5–6 | 13 |
| 7 | 14 |
| 8 | 15 |
| 9–13 (King's League) | 16 |

Matches use your own card levels, capped by League, so a card below the cap plays at its own level. A lineup is only as strong as the cards you've levelled close to your League's cap.

## Building the Duel Deck

Three decks need 24 different cards and four need 32. The Duel Deck shows in the top-right corner of your deck screen, and the reworked Deck Wizard can build a deck from your Collection, build one around a win condition you pick, and takes the cards in your other decks into account when recommending cards.

Start from three win conditions you already play well and that don't compete for the same support cards. Defensive cards and spells are the tightest constraint. If one deck takes every reliable air defence and your best spells, the other two will have obvious holes, and a best-of-3 only needs one hole to cost you the Duel. Spread your answers to air, tanks and swarms across all three decks before tuning any single one.

A fourth deck is optional. Supercell says more decks give you more flexibility to counter your opponent, but it costs 8 more unique cards, so build it once your collection can fill it with cards near your League's cap.

## Tournament schedule

Every tournament is open for 72 hours, from Friday 09:00 UTC to Monday 09:00 UTC. Seasons follow a fixed pattern:

| Season length | Weekends |
|---|---|
| 4 weeks | Duel, Duel, Grand Duel, break |
| 5 weeks | Duel, Duel, Duel, Grand Duel, break |

On Duel Weekends your run ends at 8 wins or 3 losses, and you collect rewards when it ends. Grand Duels change by League:

- **Leagues 1–8**: the run ends at 12 wins or 4 losses.
- **King's League**: up to 20 battles, ranked on a leaderboard. Wins are capped, so ties are common. They're broken by fewer losses, then more individual net battles won across your Duels, then stronger opponents beaten, then reaching your final score first.

Grand Duel rewards arrive in the days after the tournament ends.

## Rewards

Duel Weekends pay out random Common, Rare and Epic cards (Legendary and Champion in higher Leagues), Wild Cards, Gold, and a Banner Frame, Gold Banner Frame or Full Banner depending on your wins. Grand Duels in Leagues 1 to 8 pay random cards, Wild Cards and Gold, with no banners. King's League Grand Duel rewards depend on your leaderboard bracket, from 1st to 10,001+, and include Pass Royale, Gems, Gold and Wild Cards; Pass Royale and Gems appear only there. Supercell's announcement has the full tables.

## Preparing for your first weekend

Treat the first Duel Weekend as a test of the whole lineup. Note which deck loses to which kind of pressure before you change cards, and fix a recurring weakness with a swap that doesn't strip a key card from another deck.

Spend upgrades where the cap lets them count. Levelling a card that sits below your League's cap improves the deck it's in; levelling a card that's already at the cap does nothing in that League until you move up. Check the cap again whenever your League changes.

In the King's League, the second tiebreaker is individual net battles won across your Duels. By that measure a 2–0 Duel counts for more than a 2–1, which can split players tied on wins and losses, so finishing Duels cleanly is worth it there.
